01 — Our Media World

Where does information live?

Media is part of everyday life. We encounter it while studying, traveling, talking with friends, watching videos, reading news, and scrolling online. Each medium communicates differently.

READ
01

Print

Books, newspapers, magazines, brochures, and other physical publications.

WATCH / LISTEN
02

Broadcast

Television and radio that deliver information to audiences through scheduled programs.

SEARCH
03

Digital

Websites, online articles, email, e-books, and other electronically delivered information.

INTERACT
04

Social / New Media

Platforms where users can consume, create, comment, remix, and share content.

EXPERIENCE

03 — The Information Trail

Follow the trail, not the crowd.

A responsible media user does not judge information only by likes, views, or comments. Follow the information back to its source.

01

STOP

Pause before reacting or sharing.

02

TRACE

Find who created the claim and where it started.

03

COMPARE

Look for the same information from credible, independent sources.

04

DECIDE

Share only when the information is supported and useful.

Quick credibility questions

Who is the author? When was it published? What evidence is provided? Is the source qualified? Does another reliable source confirm it? Is important context missing?

04 — Media Influence

Media does more than inform us.

Media can influence what we notice, what we discuss, what we buy, and how we understand events. Its influence can be helpful, but it can also create pressure or confusion.

TIKTOK EXAMPLE

Why TikTok matters in MIL

TikTok is a form of new media where users can receive and create information. Educational videos can make concepts easier to understand, while misleading clips can spread quickly.

Key lesson: popularity is not the same as credibility.

05 — Create Responsibly

If you can post it, you can affect someone with it.

Media literacy includes responsible creation. Before uploading, commenting, or forwarding something, consider its effect on other people.

01

Respect privacy

Do not share someone's personal information or image without appropriate permission.

02

Credit creators

Acknowledge sources and creators instead of presenting their work as your own.

03

Check before sharing

Do not help misinformation travel just because a post is interesting.

04

Think about impact

Words, photos, videos, and comments can affect real people.

05

Be respectful

Disagree with ideas without attacking or humiliating people.

06

Protect your digital footprint

Remember that online actions can become part of your long-term digital identity.

06 — My Takeaway

What kind of media user am I?

Media and information literacy is not simply knowing how to use technology. It is knowing how to question information, recognize its influence, communicate responsibly, and make thoughtful choices.
